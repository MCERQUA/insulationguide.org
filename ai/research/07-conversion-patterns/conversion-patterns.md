# Conversion Patterns — CTA, Trust-Signal & Funnel Design

CCA's Get Matched service is real: "Get Matched with a Vetted, Insured Insulation Contractor"
is an established capability (same model as azinsulationremoval.com), not a marketplace-
honesty hedge. Conversion design should reflect that confidence — direct, specific claims,
not vague "we'll try to help" language.

## Core funnel logic

This site is an educational, earlier-funnel hub (per profile.md) — most traffic arrives
informational-intent (types, R-value, DIY questions). The conversion pattern is therefore
**earn-then-ask**: establish real educational value first, then offer the CTA at the moment
the reader's own content-consumption signals readiness (end of a pros/cons section, after a
cost breakdown, after a "should I DIY this" checklist) — never interrupting the first
educational payoff of a page.

## CTA placement rules

1. **Never above the fold on pillar pages.** The hero of a pillar page is 100% educational
   framing. The Get Matched CTA is a secondary hero element at most, never the sole hero CTA
   on Types/R-Value/DIY pillars (Home and Get Matched pages are exceptions — see their briefs).
2. **Cost & ROI pillar carries the highest CTA density** — it's the pillar closest to
   commercial intent (highest CPCs in the whole keyword set live here), so a CTA after the
   "what drives your quote up or down" section and again at page end is appropriate.
3. **Trust-concern content is always followed by a CTA**, not just information. The spray-foam
   reputation section (Types pillar, Blog Post 4) resolves its own tension by pointing at
   vetted/insured installation as the actual fix — CTA placement there is a natural payoff,
   not a bolt-on.
4. **DIY vs. Pro pillar's CTA follows the decision checklist**, not the DIY how-to sections —
   readers doing genuine DIY research shouldn't be interrupted mid-instructions; the CTA
   belongs right after "how to tell your project needs a pro."
5. **Every page gets at least one CTA, no page gets more than two** (except Get Matched
   itself and Home, which are conversion-primary pages by design).

## CTA copy patterns

- **Primary CTA button text:** "Get Matched with a Vetted, Insured Contractor" (full phrase,
  not shortened to generic "Get a Quote" or "Contact Us" — the specificity IS the trust
  signal).
- **Contextual CTA lead-ins** (vary by placement, always tie back to the content just read):
  - After cost content: "See what this actually costs for your home — get matched with a
    vetted, insured local contractor for a real quote."
  - After the DIY-vs-pro checklist: "If your project checked any of those boxes, get matched
    with a vetted, insured insulation contractor near you."
  - After the spray-foam trust/reputation content: "This is exactly why vetted, insured
    installation matters — get matched with a contractor who does it right."
  - Generic/soft (Home, About): "Ready to talk to someone? Get matched with a vetted, insured
    insulation contractor."
- **Avoid:** urgency/scarcity language ("Limited spots!", "Act now!") — inconsistent with the
  approachable, non-sales brand tone established across the educational content. Avoid vague
  hedge language ("we'll try to connect you with someone") — CCA's matching capability is real
  and should be stated plainly.

## Trust signals (what to show, not just claim)

1. **"Vetted and insured" defined concretely** (Get Matched page, section 4 in its brief) —
   licensing verification and insurance verification named explicitly, not left as a vague
   adjective. Vague trust claims are a competitor weakness (NAIMA's "hire a specialist" never
   explains what vetting even means) — being concrete here is real differentiation.
2. **CCA parent-brand credibility** (About page) — an established agency operating multiple
   real insulation-industry properties (sprayfoamscience.com, foammedic.com,
   azinsulationremoval.com, insulationcertificate.com) is evidence of genuine industry depth,
   not a single anonymous lead-gen site — state this plainly in About and lightly in Home's
   trust block.
3. **Neutral-guide credibility loop** — because the CTA copy and About page explain CCA earns
   through contractor-matching, not material sales, the site's demonstrated neutrality
   throughout the pillar content (no single "best" material, honest pros/cons, honest
   spray-foam-reputation discussion) becomes a trust signal in itself, reinforcing the CTA
   rather than undercutting it. This loop (neutral content → explained business model → CTA)
   is the core differentiator vs. every competitor reviewed in `03-competitor-analysis/`.
4. **Canonical contact info repeated consistently** — phone (844-967-5247), email
   (josh@contractorschoiceagency.com) — on Get Matched, About, Contact, and footer sitewide,
   so a reader who wants to just call never has to hunt for the number.
5. **Low-friction alt path** — Contact page and footer phone link exist specifically so
   readers who distrust web forms have an equally credible path to the same real service.

## Funnel summary by page type

| Page type | Primary job | CTA intensity |
|---|---|---|
| Home | Route to the right pillar | Light (secondary CTA block, mid-page) |
| Pillar pages (4) | Educate fully; earn the CTA | Medium (1-2 well-placed CTAs, contextual copy) |
| Blog posts | Deepen trust on a specific question | Light-medium (1 CTA, contextual to the post's angle) |
| Get Matched | Convert | High (this IS the conversion page) |
| About | Reinforce trust for CTA-hesitant readers | Light (single soft CTA) |
| Contact | Alt conversion path | Light (redirect callout to Get Matched for contractor-seekers) |

## What NOT to do (anti-patterns from competitor research)

- Don't bury the CTA across disconnected pages the way the government sites do (ENERGY
  STAR/EPA) — every pillar page should have its own clear, contextual CTA, not rely on the
  reader finding a separate "contact us" page.
- Don't let the CTA read as product-sales-driven the way retailer guides do ("Shop
  Insulation") — the CTA is about connecting to a *person* (a vetted contractor), never about
  a product purchase.
- Don't leave the CTA soft/vague the way NAIMA's "ask your builder" is — CCA's service is
  concrete and should be named and explained concretely every time.
- Don't gate the actual advice behind the CTA (no "sign up to see the full R-value chart") —
  the educational content stays fully free and complete; the CTA is for the next real-world
  step (hiring someone), not for unlocking information.
