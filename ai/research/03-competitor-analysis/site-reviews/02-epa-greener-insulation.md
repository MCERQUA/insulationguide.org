# Competitor Review: EPA — Identifying Greener Insulation (epa.gov)

**URL reviewed:** epa.gov/greenerproducts/identifying-greener-insulation
**Type:** Federal government resource (EPA Sustainable Marketplace section)
**Domain authority:** Very high (.gov)

## Structure
- Standard gov layout: breadcrumbs, sidebar nav, footer link farm
- Short intro on energy conservation + health/comfort benefits of insulation
- "Sustainability Considerations" section: thermal performance (R-value), toxin reduction
  (formaldehyde, isocyanates, flame retardants, VOCs), recycled content/embodied energy,
  sourcing impact, end-of-life recyclability
- Large "Additional Resources" link list (10+) to other federal tools, ENERGY STAR, GSA
  procurement standards, life-cycle assessment models, specific materials, asbestos warnings

## Strengths
- Genuinely unique angle: environmental/health lens on material choice, which almost no
  commercial competitor covers honestly (chemical content, off-gassing, embodied carbon)
- Strong trust signal for the "greener/eco-friendly insulation" long-tail cluster
- Neutral, non-promotional — doesn't push any brand or material

## Weaknesses / gaps
- Extremely thin as a standalone page — mostly a link directory, not a guide someone reads
  start to finish
- No cost data, no R-value chart, no installation guidance
- No CTA of any kind beyond a generic contact form — no funnel, no "what do I do next"
- Buried inside a huge bureaucratic site; hard to find via site navigation, only really
  reachable via search

## Tone
Neutral, technical, cautious — written for compliance/procurement readers as much as
homeowners.

## Takeaway for insulationguide.org
The environmental/health angle (VOCs, off-gassing, recycled content, eco-friendly options
like denim/wool) is real search volume (`eco friendly insulation` 4,400/mo) and almost
nobody covers it in a homeowner-friendly way. Worth a dedicated section inside "Types of
Insulation Explained" (or a future blog post) that translates EPA's dry criteria into plain
buying guidance — genuine differentiation with near-zero competition from consumer sites.
