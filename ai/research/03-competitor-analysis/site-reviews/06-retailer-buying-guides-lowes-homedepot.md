# Competitor Review: Retailer Buying Guides — Lowe's & Home Depot

**URLs reviewed (via SERP + cached snippets; both sites return HTTP 403 to automated fetch,
so this review is based on Google-indexed titles/snippets/meta descriptions plus general
familiarity with big-box buying-guide templates):**
- lowes.com/n/buying-guide/insulation-buying-guide
- lowes.com/n/buying-guide/batt-insulation-buying-guide
- lowes.com/n/buying-guide/batt-vs-roll-insulation
- lowes.com/n/how-to/install-insulation
- lowes.com/n/how-to/how-to-increase-insulation-r-values
- homedepot.com/c/ab/types-of-insulation
- homedepot.com/c/ab/insulation-r-values
- homedepot.com/b/Building-Materials-Insulation (category/product page)

**Type:** Big-box retailer buying-guide content (SEO + product-discovery funnel)
**Domain authority:** Very high (massive retail domains, huge organic footprint)

## Structure (typical of this content type, confirmed by indexed titles/snippets)
- Short buying-guide article (materials chart, R-value basics, where/how to use) that exists
  primarily to route traffic into the product category and specific SKUs
- Separate narrow guides split by material/format (batt vs. roll, specific R-value guide,
  install how-to) rather than one comprehensive pillar
- "How to install" content is genuinely useful DIY step-by-step, but written to sell the
  tools/materials needed for the job
- R-value guide framed around "how to calculate how much insulation to buy," i.e., purchase
  quantity, not decision-making

## Strengths
- Massive domain authority and site-wide internal linking = these pages rank extremely well
  regardless of content depth
- Genuinely practical DIY installation how-to (measurements, tool lists, step-by-step)
- Chart format for material comparison is fast to scan

## Weaknesses / gaps
- **Explicitly product-sales-driven** — every guide exists to route to "Shop Insulation," not
  to give neutral advice; pros/cons of a material are filtered through what the store sells
- No unbiased "should you hire a pro" framing — professional installation isn't in their
  interest to recommend (it competes with product sales)
- Content fragmented across many thin guides instead of one authoritative resource
- No real cost-per-project or ROI content beyond material list-price implications
- No path to a vetted, insured installer — the entire funnel terminates at "add to cart"

## Tone
Practical, transactional, retail-brand-safe. Friendly but unmistakably sales-oriented.

## Takeaway for insulationguide.org
Retailers own the "how to install it yourself" long-tail with strong domain authority we can't
out-rank head-on for competitive terms, but they structurally cannot give unbiased "should you
even DIY this" advice or offer a real professional-matching path — everything routes to a
shopping cart. Our DIY-vs-Professional pillar should be genuinely neutral (some jobs really
are DIY-appropriate; batts and some rigid foam) while being honest about where DIY becomes
risky (spray foam off-gassing/mixing errors, attic ventilation mistakes, crawl space moisture)
and routing those cases to Get Matched — a credibility angle retailers can't credibly claim.
