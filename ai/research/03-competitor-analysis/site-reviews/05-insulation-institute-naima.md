# Competitor Review: Insulation Institute / NAIMA (insulationinstitute.org)

**URL reviewed:** insulationinstitute.org/im-a-homeowner/about-insulation/insulation-types-comparing-insulation-options/
(+ health-safety page, homeowner section)
**Type:** Trade-association site (North American Insulation Manufacturers Association)
**Domain authority:** Moderate-high; strong subject-matter trust within the industry

## Structure
- Dual-audience nav split: homeowners vs. building/facility professionals
- Opens with a deliberately counterintuitive hook ("there is no such thing as a 'best
  insulator'") — sets a neutral, non-hyped tone immediately
- Four material types with origin/composition + application areas
- Comparative tables by performance attribute: acoustic ratings (NRC/STC), recycled-content
  %, fire-resistance temperatures
- Risk/benefit analysis section covering installation quality control and health
  considerations
- Dedicated mold/moisture section with an explicit disclaimer that "no product can prevent
  mold" — installation quality framing
- 40+ topic tags (sustainability, building science, acoustic performance, etc.) for
  cross-navigation
- Accordion "See More" pattern to manage content density

## Strengths
- **Most explicitly neutral voice in the entire competitive set** — deliberately avoids
  crowning a "best" material and instead frames installation quality as the real variable
- Directly addresses acoustic performance (NRC/STC) — a real, underserved keyword angle
  (`soundproof insulation` 5,400/mo, `best insulation for soundproofing walls` 720/mo)
- Directly addresses the "installation quality matters more than material" theme that shows
  up in the spray-foam-reputation PAA cluster (insurance/buyer pushback questions)
- Cites independent research/regulatory bodies rather than manufacturer marketing claims

## Weaknesses / gaps
- No cost/pricing information
- No climate-zone or R-value-per-inch quick-reference table on this page
- Dense, accordion-heavy UI — harder to skim than This Old House's straight pros/cons format
- CTA is soft-conversational ("ask your builder," "hire a specialist") with no actual
  path to a vetted contractor — it gestures at the exact gap CCA's Get Matched service fills
- As a manufacturer trade group, has an inherent (if soft) incentive to defend fibrous
  insulation broadly even while claiming neutrality

## Tone
Neutral-educational, deliberately non-hyped, slightly academic.

## Takeaway for insulationguide.org
NAIMA proves "installation quality > material choice" is a credible, trade-association-backed
framing — directly useful for defusing the spray-foam-reputation PAA questions honestly rather
than avoiding them. Its biggest weakness (soft CTA that never closes the loop to an actual
vetted installer) is exactly the gap CCA's real "Get Matched with a Vetted, Insured Insulation
Contractor" service fills — we can say explicitly what NAIMA only gestures at: "ask your
builder" → "get matched with a vetted, insured contractor near you."
