# Competitor Pattern Analysis

Synthesis across 6 reviewed competitors: ENERGY STAR (gov), EPA Greener Insulation (gov),
GreenBuildingAdvisor (building-science publisher), This Old House (media brand), Insulation
Institute/NAIMA (trade association), and Lowe's/Home Depot buying guides (retailers). Full
reviews in `site-reviews/`.

## The field splits into three archetypes

**1. Government/institutional (ENERGY STAR, EPA, NAIMA)**
Maximum trust, zero sales pressure, zero commercial funnel. Content is scattered across many
disconnected URLs rather than living in one cohesive guide. No pricing anywhere. Soft or
absent CTAs ("ask your builder," a generic contact form, a product-certification finder).

**2. Media/publisher (This Old House, GreenBuildingAdvisor)**
Best writing and structure. TOH nails the entry-level pros/cons format and full-journey
coverage (types → placement → install → mistakes) in one page — the strongest tonal/structural
model in the set. GBA nails technical accuracy (thermal bridging, vapor permeability,
DOE climate-zone data) but pitches too high for a true beginner and increasingly paywalls
depth. Neither has cost data or a real next-step beyond a newsletter/membership CTA.

**3. Retailer (Lowe's, Home Depot)**
Highest domain authority, most practical DIY how-to content, but every page is structurally a
product funnel — pros/cons are filtered through what's on the shelf, and "should I even DIY
this" is never asked honestly because the retailer's interest is a completed purchase, not a
correctly insulated house.

## Cross-cutting gaps (true across ALL 6 competitors)

1. **No one combines type + R-value + cost + climate-zone + DIY-vs-pro in one pillar-page
   experience.** Every competitor scatters this across multiple disconnected pages/URLs, or
   simply omits categories they have no incentive to cover (retailers skip "should I hire a
   pro," gov sites skip cost, publishers skip cost).
2. **Nobody offers a genuine "get matched with a vetted, insured installer" path.** CTAs
   across the set are: shop-now (retailers), newsletter/membership (publishers), generic
   contact form or "ask your builder" (institutions). This is a structural gap every single
   competitor shares, and it's exactly CCA's real capability.
3. **The spray-foam-reputation concern is real and mostly unaddressed.** PAA data surfaced
   consumer-trust questions ("why do insurance companies not like spray foam," "why are buyers
   walking away from homes with spray foam") that NONE of the 6 competitors address head-on
   on their core comparison pages. NAIMA gets closest with its "installation quality, not
   material, is the real risk factor" framing, but stops short of an actionable next step.
4. **Cost/ROI content is thin or absent everywhere except retailer product pages** (which
   only show material list price, not installed cost or ROI). This is the single biggest
   content gap relative to the search volume it commands — `attic insulation cost` (9,900/mo,
   $9.94 CPC), `insulation contractors near me` (18,100/mo, $18.25 CPC), and `crawl space
   insulation` (14,800/mo, $22.02 CPC) are among the highest-value terms in the whole keyword
   set and are underserved by genuinely helpful, non-sales content.
5. **Eco-friendly/health-conscious material content (VOCs, off-gassing, recycled content,
   denim/wool insulation) exists only on EPA's page, is thin, and is written for a compliance
   audience, not a homeowner.** Real search volume exists (`eco friendly insulation` 4,400/mo,
   `sheep wool insulation` 5,400/mo, `recycled denim insulation` 1,600/mo) with essentially no
   consumer-friendly competitor content.
6. **Acoustic/soundproofing use-case content is thin.** NAIMA is the only one that engages
   with NRC/STC acoustic ratings; nobody translates that into plain "what insulation actually
   reduces noise" homeowner guidance despite real volume (`soundproof insulation` 5,400/mo).

## Structural pattern worth adopting

This Old House's page skeleton (Intro → Materials with pros/cons → Specialized options →
Choosing by climate/decision-criteria → Placement by location → Install method (DIY vs pro) →
Common mistakes → Related links) is the best information architecture found in the competitive
set and maps almost directly onto this site's planned pillar structure — see
`04-content-strategy/` and `05-topical-map/`.

## Tone benchmark

TOH and NAIMA together define the tonal target: approachable and non-hyped like TOH, honestly
neutral about "no single best material" like NAIMA — as opposed to ENERGY STAR's spec-sheet
dryness, GBA's expert-only density, or the retailers' product-funnel salesiness.
