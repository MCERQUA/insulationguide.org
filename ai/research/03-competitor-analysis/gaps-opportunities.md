# Gaps & Opportunities — What insulationguide.org Can Do Better

## 1. One comprehensive pillar experience instead of scattered pages
Every competitor forces the reader to hop between disconnected URLs to piece together type +
R-value + cost + climate + DIY-vs-pro guidance. **Opportunity:** four tightly cross-linked
pillar pages (Types, R-Value, Cost & ROI, DIY vs. Professional) that each stand alone AND
link to each other at the exact moment a reader needs the next piece of information (e.g., the
Types pillar's spray-foam section links straight to the Cost pillar's spray-foam-cost section
and the DIY-vs-Pro pillar's "why spray foam is professional-only" section).

## 2. A real next step — Get Matched — where every competitor stops short
No competitor offers an actual path to a vetted, insured installer. Retailers stop at "add to
cart." Publishers stop at "sign up for our newsletter." Institutions stop at "ask your
builder" or a generic contact form. **Opportunity:** CCA's genuine "Get Matched with a Vetted,
Insured Insulation Contractor" service is a real structural advantage nobody else in this
competitive set can offer — and it should appear as the honest, well-earned payoff after
educational content, not as an upfront sales pitch (see `07-conversion-patterns/`).

## 3. Address the spray-foam-trust questions honestly instead of avoiding them
PAA data shows real consumer anxiety: "why do insurance companies not like spray foam
insulation," "why are buyers walking away from homes with spray foam," "why are people
removing spray foam." None of the 6 competitors tackle this directly on their core pages.
**Opportunity:** an honest "installation quality matters more than material" section (echoing
NAIMA's framing, but going further with an actual solution) that explains these issues stem
overwhelmingly from poor installation — then naturally bridges to "this is exactly why vetted,
insured installers matter" and the Get Matched CTA. This is a genuine trust-building content
angle, not a spin move — it uses real industry information already public via NAIMA/insurance
industry commentary.

## 4. Own the cost/ROI conversation with real numbers
Cost content is the biggest quantifiable gap: `attic insulation cost` (9,900/mo), `insulation
contractors near me` (18,100/mo, $18.25 CPC — highest-value term in the set), `crawl space
insulation` (14,800/mo, $22.02 CPC), `spray foam insulation cost` (6,600/mo). Retailers only
show material list price; nobody shows installed cost ranges by material + location + typical
project size, or talks through ROI/payback period and the federal insulation tax credit in
one place. **Opportunity:** the Cost & ROI pillar becomes the single best cost resource in the
niche by combining: cost-per-sq-ft ranges by material, whole-house cost ranges, DIY-vs-pro
cost delta, ROI/payback framing, and the real federal tax credit — content none of the 6
competitors combines in one page.

## 5. Consumer-friendly eco/health-conscious material guidance
EPA covers VOCs/off-gassing/recycled content/sourcing but in dry, compliance-oriented language.
Real search volume exists for `eco friendly insulation` (4,400/mo), `sheep wool insulation`
(5,400/mo), `recycled denim insulation` (1,600/mo) with zero consumer-friendly competitor
content. **Opportunity:** a genuinely homeowner-readable eco-materials section inside the
Types pillar (and a future blog post) — translate EPA's criteria into plain "what to ask about"
guidance without greenwashing.

## 6. Acoustic/soundproofing use-case content
Only NAIMA engages with acoustic ratings (NRC/STC), and only for a technical audience. Real
volume: `soundproof insulation` (5,400/mo), `best insulation for soundproofing walls`
(720/mo). **Opportunity:** a plain-language "insulation and noise reduction" section/FAQ —
low competition, genuine reader value, natural fit inside the Types pillar's mineral wool
and specialty-materials coverage.

## 7. True entry-level tone without sacrificing accuracy
GBA has the right facts (climate-zone R-value tables, thermal bridging, vapor permeability)
pitched too high; TOH has the right tone pitched at the right level but lighter on hard data
and paywalling depth; retailers have neither the tone nor the neutrality. **Opportunity:**
borrow GBA's factual rigor, present it at TOH's reading level, keep it fully free/ungated, and
be explicitly, credibly neutral about "there's no single best material" (NAIMA's framing) —
occupying the exact center none of the six competitors currently holds alone.

## 8. Bridge to CCA's deeper niche properties — a pathway nobody else has
Once a reader has narrowed their interest (e.g., decided spray foam is likely their answer, or
lives in Arizona and needs removal-related guidance), no competitor has anywhere further to
send them. **Opportunity:** "learn more" pathways from this hub into sprayfoamscience.com
(deep spray-foam building science), foammedic.com (post-install problem diagnosis), and
azinsulationremoval.com (AZ-specific removal) — a genuine funnel deepening that only exists
because CCA operates multiple properties in this space. See `05-topical-map/` for how this is
architected even where not all cross-links are buildable yet.

## Net positioning statement
insulationguide.org's differentiation is **completeness + honesty + a real next step**: the
only resource in this competitive set that combines accurate, entry-level-friendly coverage of
types, R-value, cost, and DIY-vs-pro in one cohesive hub, addresses real consumer trust
concerns (spray foam reputation) directly instead of avoiding them, and — unlike every
government, publisher, or retailer competitor reviewed — actually connects the reader to a
vetted, insured contractor when they're ready, plus a path deeper into CCA's specialist
insulation properties as their research narrows.
