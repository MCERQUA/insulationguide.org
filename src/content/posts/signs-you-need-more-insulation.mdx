High utility bills, uneven room temperatures, and drafts are common — but they don't always
mean the same thing, and insulation isn't always the fix. Here's how to tell what's actually
going on before you spend money.

## Common Signs of Under-Insulation

- **High utility bills relative to your home's size** — especially a noticeable jump compared
  to previous years without a change in usage habits.
- **Uneven room temperatures** — rooms far from the thermostat feeling noticeably colder in
  winter or hotter in summer.
- **Cold floors or walls** — surfaces that feel cold to the touch even when the room air
  temperature seems fine.
- **Ice dams** — ridges of ice forming at roof edges in winter, often a sign that heat is
  escaping through the attic and melting snow unevenly.
- **Drafts** — noticeable air movement near outlets, baseboards, windows, or doors.

If you're seeing more than one of these, it's worth investigating further before assuming
insulation is the answer.

## Quick DIY Checks

**Attic visual depth check.** If you can see the tops of your attic floor joists because the
insulation doesn't cover them, you likely don't have enough. This is a rough but genuinely
useful first check almost anyone can do safely from an attic access point.

**Consider a professional energy audit.** For a more precise picture, a home energy audit
(sometimes offered by utility companies, sometimes by contractors) uses tools like a blower
door test and thermal imaging to pinpoint exactly where you're losing energy — insulation gaps,
air leaks, or both.

## How Much Insulation Is "Enough"?

The honest answer depends on your climate zone. The Department of Energy publishes
recommended R-value targets by region for attics, walls, and floors — rather than duplicate
that table here, see our full breakdown in [Understanding R-Value](/understanding-r-value),
which includes the climate-zone recommendations and what they mean in practice.

## Insulation vs. Air Sealing: An Important Nuance

This is a genuinely important distinction that's easy to miss: **insulation and air sealing
solve different problems.** Insulation slows heat transfer through a material. Air sealing
stops air (and the heat or cooled air it carries) from moving through gaps and cracks
entirely. A home can have plenty of insulation and still feel drafty and waste energy if air
sealing wasn't addressed first — caulking, weatherstripping, and sealing penetrations around
pipes and wiring.

In practice, the two usually go together: a thorough insulation project typically includes air
sealing as a first step, precisely because adding insulation over unsealed air leaks doesn't
fully solve the problem. If a contractor proposes insulation work without mentioning air
sealing, it's a fair question to ask about.

## When to Call a Professional

If your DIY attic check shows genuinely thin or uneven coverage, or if the symptoms above are
significant (real comfort issues, high bills, ice dams), it's worth getting a professional
assessment rather than guessing. A contractor can measure your actual R-value, check for air
leaks, and give you a real number for what a fix costs — see our
[Insulation Cost & ROI](/insulation-cost-and-roi) guide for realistic cost ranges.

## FAQ

**How do I know if I need more attic insulation?** A visual check (can you see the tops of the
joists?) is a quick first sign, but a professional assessment gives you an exact R-value
reading and confirms whether air sealing is also needed.

**Can insulation alone fix drafts?** Not always — drafts are often an air-sealing issue as much
as an insulation one. Both usually need to be addressed together for real results.
